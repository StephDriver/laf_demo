---
title: How to check if a path is public
summary: Find out whether a path is a public right of way, and what to do if you are not sure.
---
Not every path you can see on a map or on the ground is a public right of way.

## Look at the map

Rights of way are shown on Ordnance Survey maps and on our [map](/map/). These
are guides only. The legal record is the **Definitive Map and Statement**,
held by the highway authority. See the [glossary](/glossary/).

## Look on the ground

Public rights of way are often marked with coloured arrows called waymarks:

- yellow for footpaths
- blue for bridleways
- plum for restricted byways
- red for byways open to all traffic

A path with no waymark may still be public. A path with a "private" sign may
still be a public right of way. Signs are not proof either way.

## Ask the highway authority

To be sure, ask the highway authority to confirm the status of the path from
the Definitive Map. Many authorities let you view the map online or ask to see
it in person.

## If you are still not sure

- The path may be a **permissive path**, which the landowner allows you to use.
  It is not a public right of way and can be closed.
- The land may be **open access land**, where you can walk freely. Check the
  [MAGIC map](https://magic.defra.gov.uk/).
- If you think a path should be a public right of way, the highway authority
  can tell you how to apply to have it added to the Definitive Map.
